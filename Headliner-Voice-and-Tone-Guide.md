# **Headliner Music Academy**

**Voice & Tone Guide**

*Part of the Headliner marketing design system. Revised June 2026\.*

This guide captures how Headliner sounds in writing, drawn from the copy across the site. Use it as the reference for any new marketing copy, page, email, or post. It reflects who we are: a humble, family centered music school where students grow at their own pace, with teachers beside them the whole way.

**We teach students well, and we stick with them.**

That’s the line underneath everything. We meet students where they are, teach what they need next, and stay beside them while they build. Private lessons matter here just as much as bands, camps, and performances.

# **2\. Voice: our personality**

Voice is fixed. It’s the same whether we’re writing a headline or a policy. Six traits define it.

**Warm and human.** We talk like a real person who teaches here, not a brochure. Faculty bios close on a human detail. The warmth lives in the specifics, not in adjectives.

**Humble and grounded.** We’re never above anyone, and we don’t boast. No ego, and no language that puts a student above the people around them. Teachers state their experience plainly. Everyone here stays humble, ourselves included.

**Supportive and mentoring.** Students always have a teacher beside them. We guide, we coach, we work through things together. Lean on “we’ll help” rather than “you’re on your own.”

**Reassuring.** Nerves are part of learning music, and we face them together. The energy is gentle and steady: “we can do this.” We don’t pretend the fear away, and we don’t make a big deal of it either.

**Grounded and specific.** Concrete beats vague every time. Real dates, real teachers, real instruments. Specifics are how we earn trust.

**Minimal and sincere.** Plain language. One clean line. No tricks, no formulas, no trying to sound clever.

**Active and direct.** Put the subject up front and let the sentence move. Say who does what. Skip floaty phrasing, soft lead-ins, and lines that circle the point before getting there.

# **3\. Tone: how voice flexes by audience**

Voice stays constant; tone adjusts to who’s reading and what’s at stake.

**Students (teens and adults, lessons and Band Program).** Encouraging and direct. Speak plainly about what we teach, what we expect, and how we help. For example: “We’ll figure out where you are, then we’ll teach the next step.” Humble, not hype.

**Parents (Summer Camp and family audiences).** Reassuring, concrete, low anxiety, family warm. Answer the worry plainly. For example: “By the end of the week, your child will have learned a song and played it with their band.”

**Kids (when we speak to them directly).** Reassuring, nurturing, and a little sweet. Explain things simply and keep the energy gentle. The feeling is “you’re safe here, and you can do this.”

**Faculty bios.** Warm and humble. Lead with their story and their love of music, state their experience plainly without boasting, and close on a human detail. The reader should think “I’d love to learn from this person.”

**FAQ.** Descriptive and gentle. Answer plainly, stay concise, and keep a warm tone. Skip bluntness, skip double statements, and go easy on candor. A little warmth or light excitement is welcome where it fits.

**Legal, policy, and confirmations.** Plain and clear, with the same warmth. Clarity is the whole job here.

# **4\. How we write**

A few habits keep our copy sounding like us. A few hard rules keep it from sounding like everyone else, or like AI.

**Habits we keep**

> * **Write as “we,” beside the student.** Collaborative voice: “we’ll help,” “let’s,” “together,” “in your corner.”

> * **Address people warmly and personally.** “You,” “your child,” without ever putting them above anyone.

> * **Keep it minimal.** One clean, plain line. No formulas and no forced punch.

> * **Stay family friendly.** Gentle, and a little sweet for kids and parents. This is a school full of families, and warmth is the point. We welcome every kid, including those who learn differently.

**Hard rules. These do not appear in our copy:**

> * **No em dashes.** Use a comma, a period, or rewrite the sentence. This is the one we care about most.

> * **No contrast framing.** Lines like “the norm, not the exception” or “not X, but Y” read as AI. Kill them on sight.

> * **No two beat or payoff headlines.** “Show up curious. Leave as a band.” is exactly what we avoid. It tries too hard.

> * **No ego or ownership language.** “The band belongs to you,” “you’re in the driver’s seat.” We stay humble.

> * **No strong absolutes.** “We never pressure anyone,” “guaranteed,” “always.” Softer is truer.

> * **No manufactured urgency or stacked exclamation points.** Real limits stated calmly are fine, like “spaces are limited.”

> * **Don’t lean on emphasis words.** “Real” is fine when it’s literally true, but don’t hammer it.

# **5\. Vocabulary**

**Words and phrases that are us**

*support · together · in your corner · your teacher · mentor · guide · learn · grow · at your own pace · welcome · we’ll help · let’s · family · perform (for those who want to)*

**Words and phrases to avoid**

> * **Ego and ownership:** you’re in charge, the band belongs to you, you decide (with no mention of support).

> * **Strong absolutes:** never, always, guaranteed.

> * **Contrast framing:** the norm not the exception, not X but Y.

> * **Hype and filler:** world class, unforgettable, fun filled musical enrichment, take it to the next level.

> * **Manufactured urgency:** act now, before spots run out, stacked exclamation points.

> * **Corporate filler:** synergy, leverage, solutions, robust, seamless.

**Punctuation and mechanics**

> * **Em dashes:** never. The one we care about most.

> * **Exclamation points:** sparing. A little warmth for kids and parents is fine, with no pile ups.

> * **Contrast constructions:** avoid entirely.

> * **Headlines and body:** sentence case, plain and minimal.

**Humanizer rules for marketing copy**

> * **Lead with the real thing.** Name what happens in the room: scales, warmups, song work, rhythm, ear training, technique, feedback, recital prep, band prep.

> * **Use active voice first.** “We teach,” “students build,” “teachers guide,” “families can choose.” Do not bury the point in passive or abstract phrasing.

> * **Cut floaty language.** Avoid path, journey, unlock, tailored experience, passion for music, thrive, transform, and similar filler unless the line is rewritten into something concrete.

> * **If any music school could say it, it is too generic.** Replace broad claims with a real promise, a real example, or a real teaching detail.

> * **Do not write in false balance.** Skip “some do this, others do that” unless the contrast is truly useful. Most of the time, just say what we offer.

> * **Do not soften the sentence until it disappears.** Be warm, but say the thing clearly.

> * **Sound like a teacher or front desk lead talking to a family.** Calm, specific, and confident. Never like a pitch deck. Never like ad copy.

# **6\. Do and Don’t**

Stay humble, stay supportive, and say it plainly. We guide students and grow alongside them.

| Do | Don't |
| :---- | :---- |
| All skill levels welcome. | First-timers are the norm, not the exception. (contrast framing) |
| You'll have a teacher beside you the whole way. | The band belongs to you. (ego) |
| We teach the next step clearly. | Wherever you are, there's a next step. (soft and vague) |
| We host monthly recitals and other performance opportunities. | When you're ready to perform, we'll help you get there. (floaty) |
| Spend a week learning to play together as a band. | Show up curious. Leave as a band. (tries too hard) |
| Spaces are limited, so reach out when you're ready. | Sign up today before spots run out\!\!\! (fake urgency) |

# **7\. Application by content type**

**Headlines.** Minimal and plain. One clean line. No formulas and no payoff words.

**Subheads and taglines.** One simple, warm line. For parents, make it concrete. For students, speak to growth.

**Body copy.** Second person, with “we” alongside. Short, plain sentences. Lead with how we’ll support the reader, then the concrete detail. Keep paragraphs short.

**FAQ.** Ask the question the way a parent or student really would. Answer plainly and gently, stay concise, and skip the bluntness and the double statements.

**Faculty bios.** Their story and love of music, experience stated plainly, one human detail to close. Humble throughout.

**CTAs.** Short, warm, and inviting: Request Lessons, Enroll Now, Reach Out, Learn More. No urgency and no pressure.

**Microcopy and captions.** Plain and helpful, with a warm tone.

# **8\. Voice and design**

The verbal and visual sides reinforce each other, so writers should know the visual language.

**Type.** Archivo (heavy weight) for headlines, the bold and confident face. DM Sans for body, clean and readable, the warm human register.

**Color, and what each one does:**

> * **Espresso \#1A130F** is the grounded base for dark backgrounds and body text.

> * **Crimson \#FF0044** is a sparing accent for calls to action and the occasional highlight. Use it lightly.

> * **Teal \#00A8C8** is for quiet labels and wayfinding.

> * **Yellow \#FFDA00** is for warmth and small highlights.

> * **Cream and off white** backgrounds, with muted brown body text on light.

Let the design carry the energy so the words can stay calm and humble.

# **9\. Quick checklist**

Before any copy ships, run it against this:

> 1. Does it help the student grow and feel supported?

> 2. Is it minimal and plain, with no forced punch?

> 3. Did I trade claims and hype for concrete, honest detail?

> 4. Is it humble, with no ego and no putting the reader above anyone?

> 5. Did I cut em dashes, contrast framing, strong absolutes, and stacked exclamation points?

> 6. Does it carry family, support, and “we can do this” energy, facing the nerves together rather than pretending them away?

> 7. Does it sound like a humble, real person who teaches here?

**If it clears all seven, it sounds like Headliner.**
